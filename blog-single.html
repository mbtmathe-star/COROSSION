MIG vs TIG

Home/Blog/MIG vs TIG
Welding Tips · Jul 14, 2026

Two Processes, Different Jobs

MIG and TIG are the two welding processes we reach for most, and clients often ask which one their project needs. The honest answer is: it depends on the material, the finish you need, and how fast the job has to move.

MIG (metal inert gas) welding feeds a continuous wire electrode through the gun, making it fast and forgiving — ideal for structural steel, general fabrication and repair work where speed and strength matter more than a mirror finish.

When We Reach For TIG

TIG (tungsten inert gas) welding uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode and a separate filler rod, giving the welder far more control over the weld pool. It's slower, but it produces a clean, precise bead with minimal spatter.

  • Stainless steel and aluminum work
  • Thin-gauge material where warping is a risk
  • Visible welds where finish quality matters
  • Pipe welding on pressure systems
"The right process isn't about which is 'better' — it's about matching the weld to the material and the job."

Our Rule Of Thumb

If it's structural, thick-gauge carbon steel and needs to move fast, we default to MIG. If it's stainless, aluminum, thin-gauge, or the weld will be visible in the finished product, TIG earns its extra time. Most jobs make the choice obvious once we see the material and drawings.
